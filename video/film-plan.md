# MealScholar: film plan and recording script

**Production packet · HackUMass XIV · November 14–15, 2026**

This is the current shooting plan. It replaces the earlier 4:35 dialogue draft, not the app's behavior. `MealScholar Video Script.docx` is the editable reading copy; `MealScholar Video Script.pdf` is its matching, shareable PDF. Both are generated from this plan.

## 1. The film in one sentence

A student photographs dinner, understands what the estimate can and cannot say, then puts the phone down and eats with friends.

**Creative direction:** warm, observant, lightly cinematic. No competitor screen, invented accuracy comparison, or acted sales pitch. The plate-to-app match cut is the signature moment; the technology supports the human scene rather than taking it over.

| Deliverable | Exact edit target | Structure |
|-------------------------|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Short film | 1:00 | One meal, four short text cards, real app results, friends, end card |
| Submission demo | 2:00 | Film 0:00–1:00; narrated walkthrough 1:00–1:50; final card 1:50–2:00 |
| Reusable product clips | Three separate 8–15 s clips | Photo to ranges; clarification or source check; saved meal to Today |

The two-minute target is our production choice, **not a verified competition limit**. Confirm the current video requirements with the organizers before uploading. Keep the initial submission in before midnight; target final submission by Sunday 8:30 AM EST, ahead of the team's 9:00 AM cutoff.

### Four text cards

Use one line at a time, no more than seven words per card:

1. **A photo can't weigh your food.**
2. **So it gives you a range.**
3. **When it isn't sure, it asks. Once.**
4. **Food estimates by AI. Nutrients from USDA.**

**No-question route:** replace card 3 with **Check the match. Keep the control.** Only use the question card when this recorded analysis actually asks a question.

**End card:** MealScholar / Nutrition from a photo. Honest ranges. / **mealscholar.mannava.org**

Keep **HackUMass XIV prototype · Estimates, not medical advice** legible. Show **Sequences shortened** during any edited processing wait. A three-second film end card should use a readable URL; reserve a scannable QR code for the ten-second submission end card and test it on another phone.

## 2. Lock the proof before filming

The documented nutrient path currently uses **USDA FoodData Central**, not campus-menu nutrition. Menu browsing and photo-to-menu nutrient matching are different capabilities. Do not use “campus menu first,” recipe-specific nutrition, exact accuracy, or allergen-detection claims unless Joseph and Harsh confirm the feature is implemented and the recorded result proves it.

### Recording readiness

- Open **[mealscholar.mannava.org](https://mealscholar.mannava.org)** on the actual filming phone. Hard-reload once before recording so an old cached screen does not appear.
- With Harsh, check the current access mode and use a dedicated demo diary. Do not put signup, verification email, or onboarding in the required story while those screens remain unfinished.
- Use one real plate with three or four distinct foods and a drink. Save its original hero photo and record the app analyzing that exact file.
- As soon as the hero photo is captured, start the screen recorder and analyze it once before filming the reaction or answer. Preserve that real session, question and answer; do not rely on a later model call reproducing the same question.
- Check the real output: readable boxes, plausible food labels, visible low/likely/high ranges, USDA source, and the save-to-Today path. Do not choose a take merely because its numbers look impressive.
- If a clarifying question appears, keep its actual wording and answer truthfully. If none appears, use the source-check route below. Never insert a question or substitute results from a different plate.
- Only show a goal widget if the demo user deliberately set that goal. It is not necessary to the story. Keep weekly averages labelled as covering logged days only.
- Ask Harsh for a stable recording window. This plan does **not** freeze `main` or authorize changes to another teammate's deployment.
- Record the app build/commit, device, capture time and selected route in the handoff notes. This makes a later UI change easy to distinguish from an editing mistake.

### Minimum crew and kit

| Role | Responsibility |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Producer / performer | Gabby leads the story, permission check, continuity and final content review |
| App operator / VO | Harsh, if available; otherwise a teammate familiar with the actual build |
| Camera / continuity | One non-performing teammate: framing, exposure, shot IDs and matching the plate between takes |
| Editor | Receives all originals, builds both cuts, checks captions, sound and exports |

Confirm who is taking the camera and edit roles before the first take. Three people can cover the shoot if the editor also covers a filming role.

**Kit:** two charged phones, charging cable/power bank, lens cloth, headphones, a phone support or tripod if available, the hero plate and drink, a clean demo diary, and enough free storage for originals plus a backup.

## 3. The 60-second film: timed storyboard

Timecodes below are contiguous edit targets, not instructions to rush a take. Capture at least eight seconds per setup, including two seconds before and after the action.

| Shot | Edit time | Picture / action | Sound / type |
|-----|-------------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| 01 | 0:00–0:04 | Gabby and a friend approach the dining hall. Simple exterior; no dependence on visible breath or specific weather. | Footsteps, ambient air; music enters |
| 02 | 0:04–0:06 | Hand opens the door; warm interior light. | Door and room tone |
| 03 | 0:06–0:09 | Food lands on the hero plate. Close detail; keep the eventual portions consistent. | Serving clink |
| 04 | 0:09–0:11 | The hero drink is poured. | Pour |
| 05 | 0:11–0:14 | A friend waves Gabby over to the window table. | Room tone |
| 06 | 0:14–0:17 | Tray lands; side light across the food. | Tray clack |
| 07 | 0:17–0:19 | Over shoulder: Gabby raises the phone over the plate. | Music builds gently |
| 08 | 0:19–0:21 | Locked top-down hero plate. Capture the exact photo used in the app. | Shutter; brief musical space |
| 09 | 0:21–0:27 | Match cut to that same photo in the app. Show the real boxes and gram ranges, not invented animation. | Card 1, then card 2; about three seconds each |
| 10 | 0:27–0:30 | Small, natural reaction in window light. | Music resumes |
| 11 | 0:30–0:34 | Question route: show the actual question. No-question route: open the USDA source/check view. | Card 3 for the chosen route |
| 12 | 0:34–0:38 | Question route: Gabby checks the relevant food or drink, then answers. Otherwise: review the selected food match. | Small real action sound; no acted dialogue |
| 13 | 0:38–0:43 | Show the real result after the answer or source check. A correction is shown only if one was genuinely needed. | Hold the ranges long enough to read |
| 14 | 0:43–0:47 | Show the saved meal in Today. Include only the demo user's records. | Card 4 |
| 15 | 0:47–0:50 | Phone goes face down. | Soft table tap |
| 16 | 0:50–0:54 | Friends settle in; Gabby takes the first bite. | Natural laugh; music lifts |
| 17 | 0:54–0:57 | Warm wide of the table. Optional exterior-through-window version only if permission, timing and reflections allow it. | Music resolves |
| 18 | 0:57–1:00 | Short-film end card: product name, tagline, URL and prototype label. | Last note |

**Match-cut invariant:** shot **08 → 09**, not 09 → 10. Keep the plate, food arrangement, drink position, orientation and crop consistent. Do not eat, stir or move the plate until the hero photo and physical inserts are captured.

**For the two-minute cut:** replace the early end card in shot 18 with a quiet hold on the real app result. The single final end card belongs at 1:50, so the demo does not appear to end twice.

### If a required shot fails

- **No clarifying question:** use the source-check route in shots 11–13 and the alternative narration in the walkthrough.
- **No dining-hall filming permission:** move the same story to an approved common room or kitchen, use a meal obtained with permission, and remove recognizable dining-hall signage. Keep the product proof unchanged.
- **Poor weather or lost daylight:** use a door detail for shot 01 and an interior wide for shot 17. The story must still work without exteriors.
- **Slow network or model call:** record the real wait, shorten it transparently in the edit, and keep a working local copy of the successful screen recording for judging. Do not fake a live response.
- **Broken core result:** stop and ask the app owner to investigate. A labelled design mock-up is not a substitute for the submission's working-product proof.

## 4. The two-minute demo: exact voiceover

Record the voiceover after choosing the question or source-check route. These five ten-second beats fit into 1:00–1:50; read naturally and time a rehearsal before committing the edit. Never accelerate speech simply to force the target.

### 1:00–1:10 · Photo to foods

**Screen:** the original hero photo, food labels and estimated portions.

> Start with one photo. MealScholar uses Gemini to identify the foods and estimate portions as low, likely, and high amounts.

### 1:10–1:20 · Why ranges matter

**Screen:** hold one food's portion and nutrient ranges at a readable size.

> Those portions stay visible as ranges. A photo helps you get started, but it cannot measure every ingredient or exact weight.

### 1:20–1:30 · Data provenance

**Screen:** show the selected USDA food and its nutrition source. Do not turn all ranges into one large, apparently exact number.

> Nutrients come from USDA FoodData Central, calculated from the estimated grams. You can inspect the source and choose a better food match.

### 1:30–1:40 · One honest interaction

**Question route — screen:** show the actual question, truthful answer and recomputed result.

> If the photo needs more context, MealScholar can ask one question. Your answer updates the analysis and recalculates the result.

**No-question route — screen:** inspect the existing USDA match. Change it only if a more appropriate choice is available; show any actual recalculation.

> Here, you can review the selected USDA food and its source. If a match is wrong, choose a better one and the meal recalculates.

Use **one** version in the final cut, not both. Do not describe a source check as a correction if the match stayed the same.

### 1:40–1:50 · Diary and context

**Screen:** the saved meal in Today, then the weekly summary if the current build exposes it. Keep the logged-days label visible.

> Logged meals add up in Today and the weekly view. Weekly averages cover logged days only. Estimates are not medical advice.

If the weekly screen is unavailable in the recording build, stay on Today and use:

> Logged meals add up in Today, so you can review the day so far. These are estimates of logged meals, not medical advice.

### 1:50–2:00 · Final card

**MealScholar**  
Nutrition from a photo. Honest ranges.  
**mealscholar.mannava.org**  
HackUMass XIV prototype · Estimates, not medical advice.

Hold the URL throughout the card. A QR code is optional; it must resolve to the same tested address and scan from the exported video, not just from the editing canvas.

## 5. Shoot order: 90 minutes, no overlapping crew

This is a relative call sheet. Start only after permission, dining hours, crew availability and the live-app check are settled. Do not rely on an unverified sunset time.

| From call time | Setup / shot IDs | Exit condition |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 00–10 min | Permission and consent check, clean lenses, test sound, choose framing and route | Producer confirms location and participants are cleared |
| 10–20 min | Build the hero plate; shoot 03–04 and label the original photo | Food and drink remain untouched |
| 20–40 min | Table: 06–08; record the real analysis; then 10, 12, 15–16 | Actual question/source-check route known before reactions; food untouched until the photo and inserts are done |
| 40–50 min | Friend's wave and warm wide: 05, 17; pickup inserts | No identifiable non-consenting diners |
| 50–65 min | Exterior and door: 01–02, or the indoor alternatives | Same wardrobe, phones and props; camera team has moved together |
| 65–80 min | Clean UI pickups for 09, 11, 13–14; capture separate product clips | Use the saved result and original interaction recording, not a regenerated question; no private data |
| 80–90 min | Sound effects, 30 s room tone, continuity review and backup | Originals exist on two devices before people leave |

**Do not film in story order if it costs the plate continuity.** Shots 15–16 are recorded last at the table setup: finish the top-down photo and inserts before eating. If the hero meal changes, record a new photo and matching app sequence rather than silently mixing two meals.

**Optional blue-hour pickup:** record only after the core film is complete. Keep participants at the table while the camera moves outside; do not schedule the same operator for simultaneous exterior and interior work.

## 6. Picture and sound recipe

### Camera and light

- Prefer the phone's main lens in good window light. Use a longer lens for faces only if its low-light quality holds up. Avoid the ultra-wide lens for close faces.
- Pick one delivery frame rate. For manual capture, 4K at 24 fps is an option; test indoor LEDs and use a 1/60 shutter if 1/48 bands. If the device cannot lock exposure reliably, use its normal 30 fps mode and a matching timeline.
- Lock exposure, focus and white balance for each setup when possible. Record a five-second test and inspect it for flicker and clipped highlights.
- Use ordinary SDR/Rec.709 unless the editor has agreed to a Log workflow. Do not mix HDR clips into an SDR edit without a checked color transform.
- Wipe lenses, brace the phone, use one slow move or a locked frame, and keep two seconds of clean handles around each action.
- Place the window to the side of faces. A white sheet of paper can soften the shadow; do not cover lights or block walkways.
- Make three takes per key setup. Keep food, drink levels, sleeves, phone case, table objects and seated positions consistent.

### Screen recording

- Use the phone's native screen recorder at full resolution. Turn on Do Not Disturb; hide notifications, account details, API keys and unrelated diary entries.
- Record the actual interaction and wait. Keep an untouched recording before making a transparently shortened edit.
- Keep source labels, low/likely/high values, units and the question legible in a 16:9 frame. Crop to the relevant UI rather than shrinking the entire phone to unreadable size.
- Do not type a measured weight into the film: “start with a photo” is the point. Food-match review is a clearer correction beat.
- For a demonstration history, use only the demo account's real or clearly labelled sample entries. Do not present a seeded week as someone's actual tracked diet.

### Audio and type

- No required sync dialogue in the short film. Record tray, pour, door, footsteps and cutlery separately; capture 30 seconds of room tone.
- Record voiceover in a quiet space, about 15 cm from the microphone and slightly off-axis. Capture three takes and listen back on headphones.
- Use one track licensed for the intended public upload. Keep its license or attribution requirements in the handoff.
- Match the current brand typography when available; use a clean, readable fallback if those fonts are missing. Keep text within the middle 80% of frame width.
- Hold every text card long enough to read aloud. Use high contrast, restrained transitions, captions for narration, and no rapid flashes.

## 7. Handoff, rehearsal and final check

### File handoff

Use folders for **camera originals**, **screen originals**, **voiceover and sound**, **project**, and **exports**. Name takes with the shot ID, for example `S08_hero_T03.mov`. Keep the original hero photo beside the matching screen recording.

Include a short handoff note: selected takes and question/source-check route; app build and device; whether any wait was shortened; consent/permission status; music license; and the tested destination URL. Do not include credentials, private account details or raw location metadata in the public submission.

Do not delete originals until both cuts have been reviewed and there is a second copy.

### Five-minute rehearsal before the edit is locked

1. Read the four cards and the selected voiceover route aloud with a timer.
2. Play the cut muted: the plate, one useful result and putting the phone down must still make sense.
3. Play audio without looking: the narration must describe the screens actually used, with no unsupported menu or accuracy claim.
4. Watch on a phone at normal size. Read the ranges, units, USDA source, captions and URL without zooming.
5. Open the upload as a signed-out viewer, check sound, seek near the end, and test the final link or QR code.

### Deliverable acceptance

- [ ] The film is 1:00 and the submission cut is 2:00, or any required organizer limit has been confirmed and the cut adjusted.
- [ ] Shots 08 and 09 show the same plate and photo; the chosen clarification/source-check route stays consistent.
- [ ] All product footage is real. Any shortened wait is labelled. No invented question, numbers, goal, or restaurant match appears.
- [ ] Nutrient provenance is USDA unless a newer implemented path has been verified with the owners.
- [ ] Faces and location are cleared for filming; no UMass endorsement is implied.
- [ ] Captions match the final voiceover; music is licensed; there is no clipped speech or missing audio.
- [ ] Exports are H.264 video with AAC audio at 1080p or higher, use the chosen frame rate, and play on another device.
- [ ] Both cuts, the caption file, project, originals and permission/license notes have a backup.
- [ ] Submission links work for a signed-out viewer, and the final project URL is correct.

The checkboxes are shoot-day signoffs, not claims that filming, permissions, recording or submission have already happened.
